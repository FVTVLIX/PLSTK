# PLSTK visual direction

PLSTK is pronounced like plastic. The name suggests a material that can be shaped, which fits the experience of turning footage into an edit.

The identity should feel approachable, creative, tactile, and confident. It needs to look natural on a Mac while remaining recognizable at a small size.

## Icon concepts to explore

### Folded frame

A soft rectangular frame folds at one corner to suggest a piece of material being shaped. A small opening or cut gives it a connection to editing without relying on a generic play triangle.

### Layered ribbon

Two simple overlapping ribbons suggest a timeline and visual layers. A restrained translucent treatment could connect the icon to the name while keeping a clear silhouette.

### Sculpted P

A compact letter P formed from a continuous strip could serve as both app icon and standalone brand mark. The shape should read clearly without needing the full name.

## Visual character

Start with the editor's neutral charcoal surfaces and a light foreground. Explore one accent color rather than a large collection of gradients. A translucent material may work as a detail, but contrast and silhouette should carry the icon.

Use a wordmark that feels clear and human. Avoid tiny controls, detailed timelines, lettering inside a busy icon, or imagery borrowed from another product.

## Assets to prepare

1. Three distinct icon directions shown at both large and small sizes.
2. A selected master icon with a transparent background and an editable source.
3. macOS app icon exports and a packaged icon resource after the design is approved.
4. A wordmark, a repository image, and a simple release graphic.
5. A short record of asset authorship, source files, and licensing.

No final icon is selected yet. Generated concepts are starting points for review rather than finished brand assets. Keep private drafts in `Design/Private` and add only reviewed public assets to the repository.
